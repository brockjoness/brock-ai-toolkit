# Newsletter Voice Reference

> These are Brock's voice rules for the Clickflow newsletter. They are opinionated and specific. **Adapt them to your own voice** — the structure of the file is the reusable part. The exact rules (banned words, sign-off, paragraph rhythm) are illustrative.

This file is the tone bible for the newsletter skill. Load it before writing any email.

---

## Paragraph Rhythm

- Paragraphs can run 1-4 sentences. Vary the length so the rhythm breathes — a 4-sentence paragraph followed by a 1-sentence punch hits harder than uniform short blocks.
- Default toward shorter, but let a paragraph run longer when the ideas are connected and the flow earns it.
- Single line breaks between paragraphs. No double spacing. No indentation.
- Reads like a confident operator writing to a peer, not a marketer optimizing for scroll.
- Sentences are complete but not long. Average sentence length: 10-18 words.

---

## Opening Pattern

- Never open with a greeting. No "Hi", "Hey", "Hello", "Dear", or any name.
- Jump straight into the content. Start with a statement, observation, or story.
- Acceptable openers: "Quick update", "Something I built this week", "I spent last Tuesday doing X", or just a direct declarative sentence.
- First sentence should make the reader want to read the second sentence. That is its only job.

---

## Tone

- Conversational but not sloppy. Every sentence is intentional.
- Confident but not boastful. States facts and numbers, never brags.
- Generous. The default posture is giving things away.
- Honest about what is hard, what broke, what took longer than expected.
- No hedging language ("I think maybe", "it might be worth"). Be direct.
- No filler ("just wanted to share", "I thought you might find this interesting"). Get to the point.

---

## Word and Verb Choice

- **Concrete over generic.** Prefer specific nouns and numbers over abstract ones.
  - Bad: "takes a lot of time" → Good: "takes hours"
  - Bad: "a few brands" → Good: "150+ brands"
- **Active verbs over passive or weak ones.** Prefer "pulls / runs / builds / scrapes / cuts / launches" over "gives / does / handles / conducts / provides."
- **Tighten ruthlessly.** Every adjective must earn its place. Cut "well-written," "great," "comprehensive," "full," "really," "very," "incredibly," "actually," "literally" by default.
- **Front-load the moment.** When a sentence has a temporal trigger ("the moment you...", "right after..."), put it first, not at the end.

## Storytelling Approach (example — replace with your own anchors)

- Shares personal experience as the frame for value delivery.
- Brock's story anchors (replace with your own):
  - Agency background: managed paid media for a portfolio of D2C brands
  - Current build: Clickflow product, public AI toolkit
  - Building in public: showing the real process, including failures
- Stories are brief. 2-4 sentences to set up context, then pivot to the takeaway.
- Never tell a story without a reason. The story serves the lesson or the resource.

---

## CTA Style

- Soft only. Never urgent, never pushy, never scarcity-driven.
- Frame as an offer: "here if you want it", "grab it here", "reply and I will send it"
- Acceptable CTA formats:
  - "grab it here: [link]"
  - "if you want early access: [link]"
  - "reply to this and I will send it over"
- Never use: "Act now", "Limited time", "Don't miss out", "You need this", "Click here", "Sign up now"

---

## Sign-Off

- Brock's rule: just the first name on its own line. No "Best,", "Cheers,", "Thanks,", "— Brock", "Best regards,". No dash, no comma, no emoji after the name.
- Adapt to your own.

---

## Subject Lines

- Lowercase-feeling. Sentence case at most. Never Title Case Every Word.
- Short. Under 45 characters.
- Curiosity or benefit driven. Should feel like a text message subject, not a marketing headline.
- Examples of the style:
  - "the audit tool nobody asked for"
  - "built something this week"
  - "free notion board"
  - "what 150 brands taught me about creative"
  - "new tool — ad spend calculator"
- Never use: ALL CAPS, emojis, exclamation marks, brackets like [FREE] or [NEW]

## Banned Language (Brock's list — edit yours)

### Hype Words
- game-changer, revolutionary, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, next-level
- incredible, amazing, insane, mind-blowing, powerful
- unlock, supercharge, skyrocket, explode, crush it, kill it, smash it, dominate

### Marketing Cliches
- just wanted to share, thought you might like, hope you are doing well
- do not miss out, limited time, act now, hurry
- best-in-class, world-class, industry-leading, thought leadership
- hack, secret, trick, ninja, guru, wizard
- 10x, level up, optimize, leverage, synergy, disrupt

### Filler Phrases
- I think maybe, it might be worth, you might want to consider
- just a quick note, wanted to reach out, touching base
- without further ado, that being said, at the end of the day
- in today's fast-paced world, in this day and age

### Punctuation and Formatting
- Exclamation marks (never)
- Emojis (never)
- Em dashes (never — use a period, comma, or parentheses instead)
- ALL CAPS for emphasis
- Excessive bold (bold is acceptable for a tool name or a single key phrase)

### Sentence Construction
- No hanging dependencies. Every sentence stands on its own.
- No comma splices used for dramatic effect.
- No fragments used as one-liners unless the context genuinely earns it.

---

## Value-First Principle

Every email must pass this test: "If the reader never buys anything from me, was this email still worth opening?"

If the answer is no, the email needs more value. Add a tool, a template, a lesson, or a specific tactic.

## Reference Patterns by Email Type

### Resource Drop
```
[1-2 sentences: what I noticed or what problem this solves]

[1-2 sentences: what the tool/resource is and what it does]

[1-2 sentences: how I use it or how it came about]

[soft CTA with link]

[Sign-off]
```

### Build Update
```
Quick update.

[1-2 sentences: what changed]

[2-3 sentences: why it matters, what it means for the reader]

[1 sentence: what is coming next]

[optional soft CTA]

[Sign-off]
```

### Insight / Lesson
```
[1-2 sentences: set the scene with a specific moment or number]

[2-3 sentences: what happened, what went wrong or right]

[1-2 sentences: the takeaway]

[optional: link to a related free resource]

[Sign-off]
```

### Behind-the-Scenes
```
[1 sentence: what I spent the week on]

[2-4 sentences: the real process — what worked, what broke, what I learned]

[1 sentence: what is next]

[optional reply CTA or link]

[Sign-off]
```

---

## Length Guidelines

- Target: 150-250 words. Maximum: 300 words.
- If the draft is over 300 words, it is too long. Cut the weakest paragraph.
- Shorter is almost always better.
